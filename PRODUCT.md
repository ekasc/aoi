## Design Context

### Users
Couples who want a private home and relationship archive built from what they intentionally share. Used during evenings at home, planning dates, and remembering milestones. Us is the living relationship home. Memories is the deliberate shared archive. Camera-roll discovery and face recognition are not part of the product promise.

Free remains a complete couples app. Aoi Plus adds shared archive storage, more future letters, and PDF chapter keepsakes. Sky history, richer chapters, recaps, and capsules need future systems and are not current paid benefits. See [relationship home and archive](docs/product/relationship-home-and-archive.md) for semantics and deferred work.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Playful, intimate, calm.

The app should feel like a beautiful private journal that celebrates a relationship — joyful without being saccharine, personal without being sentimental, calm without being boring. Moments should feel special when added, not like filling a form.

**Tone**: Warm and personal. Labels like "Your moments", "Time together", "This look is for both of you" are on-brand. Keep this voice throughout.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Warm, organic, editorial. Beach/nature-inspired palettes (Sunset Shore, Sea Glass, Deep Ocean). Not digital/techy, not corporate, not clinical.
- **Theme**: Both light and dark equally supported. Light mode is warm and paper-like; dark mode is deep and cozy, not "dark mode techie."
- **Typography**: Display font (New York serif) + body font (Trebuchet) + mono meta font. The serif display font is the key personality marker.
- **Anti-references**: No neon glows, no hacker dark mode, no social-feed patterns, no engagement metrics. Explicitly NOT another productivity calendar.
- **References**: Think personal diary, printed photo album, a quiet private corner of the internet.

### Design Principles
1. **Private by default, joyful in expression** — Every interaction should reinforce that this is a safe, personal space. Delight in small moments without demanding attention.
2. **Moments deserve ceremony** — Adding a milestone or note should feel more like writing a journal entry than filling a form. Give the content room to breathe.
3. **Two people, not one** — Color-coding (accent vs partnerAccent) is a key design device. The app always knows who did what, and makes that visible with warmth, not hierarchy.
4. **Calm over engagement** — No badges, no streaks, no urgency. The interface should be restful, not demanding.
5. **Native-first, not web-ported** — Use platform affordances (iOS glass, native sheets, SF Symbols, haptics). The app should feel like it belongs on the device.
