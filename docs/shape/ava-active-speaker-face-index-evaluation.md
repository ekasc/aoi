# AVA ActiveSpeaker face-index evaluation

The pipeline ran on natural full movie frames. Held-out top-k improved positive
retrieval from 11/60 to 17/60, but missed 43 positives. Detector misses account
for 25 of those misses. The annotation-negative match counts are not reliable
wrong-person counts because AVA identifies short tracks, not actors across shots.

Decision: `fix detector/alignment first`. Detection, rather than alignment, is
the first problem demonstrated here. This result does not justify a recognition
acceptance claim or replace private couple validation.

## Annotation semantics

CSV rows have eight fields:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | `video_id`, the source YouTube video identifier |
| 2 | `frame_timestamp`, elapsed seconds from the start of the video |
| 3 and 4 | Face-box top-left x and y |
| 5 and 6 | Face-box bottom-right x and y |
| 7 | Speaking label |
| 8 | `entity_id`, linking a face track across adjacent frames in that video |

Coordinates are normalized to full-frame width and height, with top-left origin.
The actual archives use `NOT_SPEAKING`, `SPEAKING_AUDIBLE`, and
`SPEAKING_NOT_AUDIBLE`. The website describes the latter two with slightly
different spellings. All three labels count equally as face presence here.

An entity ID is stable within its annotated face track. It is not a globally
named actor or a reliable identity across interrupted tracks. The paper describes
tracks generated by a face detector and overlap/similarity tracking. Gaps below
0.2 seconds can be smoothed. Rating tracks are approximately one to ten seconds
long and can break when the shot moves away. The same visible person can therefore
have another entity ID later, even within the same scene.

The archives contain 120 train CSVs and 33 validation CSVs, totaling 3,444,621
rows and 37,738 entity tracks. That is fewer videos than the website's historical
160-video description. Observed track durations range from 0.52 to 10.13 seconds,
with a median of 2.53 seconds. The median first within-track timestamp interval
is 0.04 seconds. Individual source frame rates and timestamp increments vary.
No assumption of a universal 25 fps timeline enters frame extraction.

Training and validation designate the dataset partitions, not this benchmark's
enrollment/query split. Each case remains within one video. No model is trained
on AVA labels.

### Annotation completeness and negative ground truth

The paper explicitly notes missed candidate face detections. Human raters label
the generated tracks, rather than independently establishing exhaustive actor
presence for every frame. Track merging and false tracks can be discarded.
Consequently, missing annotations can reflect missed faces or track boundaries.

Positive frames explicitly enumerate both selected tracks. Negative frames mean
neither selected track ID is annotated, while at least one other face track is
annotated. They are **annotation negatives**, not proven actor-absent negatives.
Even a fully annotated frame can be an annotation negative after the same actors
receive new track IDs. No strong actor-negative subset was established.

## Pair selection and sampling

`scripts/prepare-ava-pair-benchmark.py` scans annotation rows, not recognition
scores. It finds 105 eligible videos under the following rules:

1. At least 20 raw annotated timestamps containing both tracks, at least 20
   containing A without B, and at least 20 containing B without A.
2. Enrollment starts at the first co-occurrence. Reference candidates are within
   its first two seconds. Both normalized face boxes must have width and height
   at least 0.06.
3. Select the first four eligible reference timestamps spaced at least 0.5 seconds
   apart. Require at least three. Both targets use these full reference frames.
4. Evaluation begins at least four seconds after the first co-occurrence. Retain
   annotated timestamps at least one second apart, through ten seconds after the
   last appearance of either selected track.
5. Require at least two downsampled positive frames. Rank candidates by sampled
   positive count, then raw co-occurrence count, then lexical identifiers.
6. Take the highest-ranked pair per video, then ten videos in the same ranking.
   One higher-ranked video exceeded the explicit 800 MB resource cap and was
   skipped. This was a resource exclusion, not a recognition failure or dead URL.

Raw observations qualified the pairs, but the temporal split leaves only six
sampled positives per case. These are short tracks. The requested 20 sampled
positives per case cannot fit in a ten-second track with one-second spacing and
a reference gap. The 20-raw-timestamp qualification does not mean 20 independent
photographs.

Actual enrollment-to-first-query gaps range from 2.43 to 2.50 seconds. Mean gap
is 2.461 seconds and median gap is 2.45 seconds. No reference timestamp or content
hash occurs in that case's query set. Query hashes are unique within each case.
Reference hashes are also distinct in this run. No duplicate query removals were
needed. Cases are selected independently of detection or embedding success.

### Full-frame extraction and integrity

`ffmpeg` seeks each requested elapsed timestamp and decodes one full frame.
`showinfo` records its actual presentation timestamp. Container start time is
subtracted before comparing that timestamp with the annotation timeline. When
an annotation falls between source frames, the first decoded frame at the seek
point is used. The extraction gate accepts at most 50 ms difference; the maximum
observed normalized difference was 30 ms.

The full decoded raster goes through Aoi's detector. There are no pasted faces,
synthetic composites, or annotation-face crops in the recognition inputs.
Diagnostic contact sheets were used only for visual review, never as inputs.

SHA-256 checks validate cached videos and frames. The evaluator independently
checks frame bytes, disjoint reference/query hashes and timestamps, two-second
minimum gap, one-second query spacing, distinct target IDs, and mechanically
derived category membership. Unavailable inputs are not counted in denominators.

## Enrollment and detector matching

Vision rectangles and annotation boxes are compared in the full-frame top-left
pixel coordinate system. Annotation fractions are multiplied by the actual
decoded width and height. Candidate edges need IoU at least 0.30. Descending IoU,
then entity ID and detection index, determines a deterministic greedy one-to-one
matching. No identity score participates in this matching.

For enrollment, only the detection geometrically matched to the reference target
supplies that person's embedding. Other detected people in the same full frame
are not enrolled as that person. They may form anonymous reference-side clusters.
This is ground-truth-assisted reference disambiguation, not ground-truth-assisted
query recognition.

Landmarks, alignment, and SFace remain unchanged. Selected references are not
replaced when detection/alignment/embedding fails. Four cases have fewer than
three usable reference embeddings on at least one side. That weak enrollment is
retained and reported rather than selecting replacements with better scores.

For `references`, only enrollment-frame embeddings create clusters. Query faces
are classified without updating those clusters. For `all`, query embeddings also
enter unsupervised clustering. Their labels are used only afterward for detector
metrics and diagnostics, not clustering or attachment. The experiments remain
separate.

## Detector ceiling and pipeline stages

| Measurement on positive frames | Count and rate |
| --- | --- |
| Annotated targets matched to Vision detections | 90/120, 75.0% |
| Matched targets with usable alignment | 89/90, 98.9% |
| Aligned targets with successful embeddings | 89/89, 100% |
| Both targets detected | 35/60, 58.3% |
| Only A detected | 15/60, 25.0% |
| Only B detected | 5/60, 8.3% |
| Neither detected | 5/60, 8.3% |
| Detector call errors | 0 |

The measured geometric detector ceiling is 35/60, or 58.3%. Baseline recall
reaches 11/35, or 31.4%, of that ceiling. Held-out top-k reaches 17/35, or 48.6%;
all top-k reaches 18/35, or 51.4%. Better detection alone would not solve the
remaining identity-matching failures.

This ceiling is an IoU-based diagnostic, not an infallible physical-face oracle.
A valid detection below the overlap cutoff is a geometric miss. Crowded boxes,
tracking inaccuracies, and rendition timing can affect the association.

uses the unchanged spike defaults: normal similarity 0.55, low-quality similarity

Held-out gain is six positives, 10.0 percentage points, or 54.5% relative recall

All mode improves over held-out by just one positive, 1.7 percentage points.

does not demonstrate a material transductive gain. Six of ten pairs have zero
positive retrieval with either primary cluster mode. A pooled improvement must
not hide them.

### Annotation-false-pair review

Every main-setting flagged frame has local ground-truth entity sets, detection
rectangles, face/cluster assignments, cluster sizes, reference scores/support,
attachment reasons, and pair-query decisions. Baseline diagnostics retain each
face's A and B reference similarities. No embeddings appear in this document.

There are 25 distinct flagged frames in the union of baseline and all eight
main-setting strategy runs. Full-frame visual comparison with enrollment frames
shows apparent person continuity in 24 of them after the target track IDs stop.
One crowded frame remains unresolved. The private review records these as
annotation-limitation diagnostics, not independent actor annotations. Ground-truth
labels and all denominators remain unchanged.

No verified actor-level false-pair rate is claimed. These reviewed continuations
cannot establish lookalike errors, identity merges, or outlier-driven errors.
Neither the annotation count increase nor the visual review is sufficient to
certify production precision.

## Reproduction and verification

```sh
python3 -B scripts/prepare-ava-pair-benchmark.py --pairs 10
node scripts/evaluate-ava-pairs.mjs
npx vitest run --maxWorkers=2
pnpm run typecheck
npx eslint scripts/evaluate-ava-pairs.mjs features/face-index/ava-ground-truth.ts tests/unit/face-index/ava-ground-truth.test.ts
python3 -B -m unittest discover -s tests/python -p test_ava_benchmark.py
```

The Mac evaluator reuses the existing Vision/SFace engine and requires its
existing local setup. It does not boot a simulator or build the app. It closes
both native/inference helpers and removes its compilation scratch directory.

Downloads, frame layout, content receipts, model-specific embedding cache,
`manifest.json`, `report.json`, and visual-review artifacts live only under
`.expo/face-benchmark/ava-active-speaker/`. Repeated preparation and evaluation
produced the same primary results. The committed artifacts are tooling, tests,
and aggregate documentation only.

Verification passed: 2,662 Vitest tests across 255 files, ten Python contracts,
TypeScript typecheck, ESLint on changed JavaScript/TypeScript files, and diff
whitespace checks. Python syntax checks also passed. No benchmark artifacts were
staged, and `git ls-files .expo` returned no files.

Tests cover annotation categories, isolated/group exclusivity, empty-frame
exclusion, deterministic spacing, distinct targets, temporal gaps, hash leakage,
duplicate query content, metadata-only reference selection, IoU, and one-to-one
geometric matching. They do not prove actor identity across broken tracks.

## Limitations and prior investigations

AVA demonstrates natural full-frame imagery, simultaneous faces, pose/lighting/
scale variation, short-gap temporal recognition, and pair-presence classification.
It does not adequately test months or years of appearance change, phone-camera
libraries, saved social-media images, couple-specific distributions, long-term
identity clustering, or cross-device media.

Only ten longest-qualified cases are evaluated. They have short temporal gaps,
no isolated-target query coverage, limited usable-reference coverage, and
correlated samples. Annotation negatives are not actor negatives. This is a
public diagnostic gate, not validation for actual Aoi users.

The prior findings remain intact:

- [Wikimedia](public-natural-face-index-evaluation.md) has insufficient identity
  ground truth. Its builder is exploratory and unused by this benchmark.
- [PIPA](pipa-face-index-evaluation.md) stopped at Flickr API/access requirements.
  No further Flickr requests were made in this AVA pass.
- AVA ActiveSpeaker is the current executable public benchmark, with the track
  limitations documented above.

## Decision gate

A. The pipeline detects and recognizes some natural multi-person full frames,
   but six pairs have zero primary positive recall. It is not reliably working.

B. Held-out top-k beats the fixed-reference baseline by six positives and ten
   percentage points in this short-gap sample. That does not establish broad
   generalization or a safe precision trade-off.

C. Held-out recall is 28.3% against a 58.3% geometric detector ceiling, about
   48.6% of the ceiling. Both detection and identity matching leave substantial
   room for improvement.

D. Detector misses are the largest supported earliest failure source, 25 of
   43 held-out misses. Alignment succeeds for 89 of 90 detected targets.

E. Cluster-first increases annotation-false pairs, but the visual review and
   track-ID semantics prevent interpreting them as confirmed wrong-actor matches.

F. Max has the best held-out positive recall. There is no defensible unique
   winner for real-person precision. The primary top-k result is retained.

G. This is not sufficient evidence to approve private couple validation as the
   next acceptance gate. Fix or investigate the demonstrated detector misses,
   then rerun without changing identity scoring to hide them. Private validation
   remains necessary after a stronger public diagnostic result.

Final decision: `fix detector/alignment first`.
